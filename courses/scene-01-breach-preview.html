The $2.4 Billion Password

In 2024, an attacker got one employee's login at Change Healthcare — which processes about a third of all U.S. patient records. Multi-factor authentication wasn't turned on, so he just logged in. He spent nine days inside the network. Then deployed ransomware.

Pharmacies couldn't fill prescriptions. Hospitals couldn't verify insurance. Total damage: $2.4 billion.

The setting that would have stopped it is free and takes about a minute.

So what stopped it?

One step that wasn't turned on

The attacker walked in at Step 2. They had firewalls, encryption, monitoring — none of it mattered, because the one thing that stops a stolen login wasn't on.

1
🔑 What's MFA?
A second step after your password — a code on your phone, or a fingerprint. Even with your password stolen, no one gets in without that second piece. Free, a minute to set up, and it blocks 99% of automated attacks.
2
🤔 Why didn't they have it?
They were in the middle of upgrading their systems. The attackers didn't wait. When systems are connected, one skipped step affects everyone downstream — that's why the basics matter even when they feel obvious.
