Why it matters

Habits

Four habits that matter more than any tool

Most incidents come down to a few repeated mistakes. These four habits address the ones that come up most.

🔍Don't fall for fake＋

Attackers copy the look of real emails and login pages — logo and layout are trivial to fake. What they can't fake is the sender's real address and the actual link destination. Check who it's really from and where the link goes. That five-second habit catches most scams, because the disguise falls apart the moment you look past the surface.

🔑One password, one account＋

When a website gets breached, stolen passwords end up in databases attackers trade. Their first move is to try those same combinations everywhere else — bank, work, social. If you reused the password, one old breach hands them everything. A unique password per account keeps a breach contained.

📢Share with care＋

Your public posts are free reconnaissance. A whiteboard in a photo reveals what systems you run; "out of office until Monday" tells them when no one's watching; a vendor's name lets them impersonate a supplier. None of it feels sensitive alone — the risk is what it hands an attacker for free. A glance before posting is usually enough.

🚨Say something＋

Most breaches have a moment where someone noticed something odd and said nothing, assuming it was nothing. That silence gives an attack time to spread. Reporting early, even a false alarm, lets IT stop something small before it becomes expensive. You're never in trouble for flagging something that turned out fine.

The attack

How $2.4B disappeared

The fix

So what would have stopped it?

Change Healthcare had firewalls, encryption, monitoring. None of it mattered because the one thing that would have blocked that login wasn't on.

🔑What's MFA?＋

A second step after your password — a code on your phone or a fingerprint. Even with your password stolen, no one gets in without that second piece. Free, takes a minute, blocks 99% of automated attacks. The course "MFA: The Second Step" walks you through it.

Daily

Three daily habits

Everything above comes down to a few things you do every day.

⚙️Keep things updated＋

Turn on auto-updates for your OS, browser, and phone. Updates patch holes attackers already know about.

🔗Think before you click＋

Hover (desktop) or long-press (mobile) to see where a link actually goes. Unexpected file? Don't open it — check with the sender through a different channel.

🔒Lock your devices＋

Phone: PIN/biometric + auto-lock under 2 min. Laptop: screen lock after 2-5 min, encryption on. Same habit at home as at the office.
