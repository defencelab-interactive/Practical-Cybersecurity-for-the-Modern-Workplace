What stands out
No flags is not the same as safe. This page only reads the address. It knows nothing about who owns the domain, when it was registered, or what the page does once it loads. A brand-new domain built for one campaign can look perfectly ordinary here. If a message is asking you for money, credentials or access, the address is never the whole check — confirm it with the sender on a channel you chose.

Checking without this page

  • Desktop: hover over the link and read the preview in the bottom-left corner of the browser.
  • Phone: press and hold the link until a preview appears, then dismiss it.
  • Read the domain right to left: find the last two parts before the first single slash. That is the site you are visiting.
  • Everything to the left of it is chosen by whoever owns that domain, and can say anything at all.

Where the name goes

In dropbox.secure-files.com the site is secure-files.com. In www.dropbox.com/share the site is dropbox.com. Same word, completely different owner — the only thing that changed is which side of the last dot it sits on.

Domain boundaries are worked out from a shortened list of public suffixes covering common endings such as .com, .co.uk and .com.ua. Unusual national suffixes may be split one label off — when in doubt, treat the highlighted part as approximate and read the full host yourself.
