Three things to change tonight
Change the router's admin password
This is not the WiFi password — it is the one that gets into the router's own settings. On a lot of routers it is still admin.
  • Type 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1 into a browser on your home network
  • The current login is often printed on a sticker on the router itself
  • Change it to something long, and save it in your password manager
Check the encryption is WPA2 or WPA3
In the same settings, find the wireless security section.
  • WPA3 is best, WPA2 is fine
  • WEP or Open means change it now — WEP can be broken in minutes, and Open means no encryption at all
  • If it offers "WPA2/WPA3 mixed", take it — old devices keep working
Update the router, and turn on automatic updates if it offers them
Routers get security patches like everything else, and almost nobody installs them.
  • Look for Firmware, Update, or System in the settings
  • If the router is more than about six years old, check whether it still gets updates at all — many stop
  • If your provider supplied it, they may update it for you. Worth confirming rather than assuming
A guest network is worth the two minutes. Most routers can run a second network with its own password. Visitors, and anything in the house that connects but never needs updating — a TV, a doorbell, a thermostat — go on that one. They then cannot reach the laptop you work from.
