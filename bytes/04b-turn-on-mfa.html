Two decisions people get wrong

Turning it on asks you two questions. These are the answers.

Which second factor

Most services offer several. They are not equivalent.

Best
A passkey or a hardware key. Tied to the real site, so a convincing fake login page gets nothing. Your phone or laptop can act as one.
Good
An authenticator app — the six-digit code. Works offline, cannot be intercepted from your phone number.
Last resort
SMS. Better than nothing, and the weakest of the three: the code goes to your phone number, which someone can get transferred to a SIM of their own.

If a service only offers SMS, take it. If it offers an app, take the app.

Where the recovery codes go

When you switch MFA on, most services show you a set of one-time recovery codes. People skip past this screen, and it is the screen that decides whether losing your phone is an inconvenience or a lockout.

Save them somewhere that is not the phone they would be recovering. Printed and in a drawer is fine. In your password manager is fine. A screenshot in your camera roll is not, because the camera roll is on the phone.

Back up the authenticator app itself as well, if it offers that. Both together, because cloud backup fails quietly if the account it syncs to was also only on that phone.

If it is a work account, you may not be the one deciding

On an account your employer manages, an administrator can require MFA, choose which methods are allowed, or hide the setting from you entirely. If the option is missing or greyed out, that is usually why — not a fault.

If someone handles IT or security where you work, that is a one-line question for them: is MFA on for my account, and which method should I use? If nobody does, the setting is yours to turn on, and the order below still holds.

Why there are no screenshots. Menu paths and screenshots go out of date within months — vendors move things, the web and the app differ, and on a managed account the option may not be where anyone else sees it. Terms change far more slowly than addresses, so this page gives you the term.
