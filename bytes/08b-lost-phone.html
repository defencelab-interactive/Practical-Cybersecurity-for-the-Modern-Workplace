When Your Phone Goes Missing

Two lists. One is three settings that only work if you turn them on beforehand. The other is the order to do things in when the phone is already gone — opened at the worst possible moment, so it is short.

~2 min
Turn on Find My Device — and test that it works
It cannot be switched on once the phone is gone, and an untested setting is a guess.
  • iPhone: Settings → your name → Find My → turn on all three options
  • Android: Settings → Google → Find My Device, and check Location is on
  • Test it: open icloud.com/find or android.com/find in a browser and confirm your phone appears on the map
Back up your authenticator, and keep recovery codes elsewhere
If your second factor lives only on the phone, losing it locks you out of the accounts you would use to recover everything else — including your email.
  • Google Authenticator: profile icon → "Use on multiple devices"
  • Microsoft Authenticator: Settings → cloud backup. Authy backs up by default
  • Save the recovery codes for your two or three most important accounts somewhere physical — not on the phone they would be recovering
Write down who you would call, and from what
Your contacts are in the phone. If it is gone, so are the numbers.
  • Your IT or security team, if the phone has work accounts on it
  • Your bank's number, from the back of the card
  • Your mobile provider, for blocking the SIM
  • Keep these somewhere reachable without the phone — on paper, or on a laptop

Nothing here is sent anywhere. Ticks keep your place while you work through it — they live in this browser only, so do not count on finding them again. The left-hand tab is the part to act on now: the day you need the right-hand one is the day you will not have this page either.
Do these in order. Each step is reversible until the last one. Tap a step to tick it off as you go.
1Lock it remotelyBlocks use immediately, shows a message and a contact number on the screen, and keeps reporting location. Reversible if it turns up in a coat pocket.
  • iPhone: icloud.com/find → select the device → Mark As Lost
  • Android: android.com/find → select the device → Secure Device
  • Add a number where you can be reached — most phones handed in are handed in by someone ordinary
2Tell whoever needs to knowIf the phone has work accounts on it, IT can cut its access from their side — which is faster and broader than anything you can do alone.
  • IT or security, for a work phone or any phone with work email on it
  • Your mobile provider, to block the SIM so it cannot receive your confirmation texts
  • Say when and where you last had it — it matters for both of them
3Change the passwords that matterStart with email, then banking, then anything else that was signed in. Email first, because it is the route back into the rest.
  • Do it from a different device, not from a borrowed phone
  • If your second factor was on the lost phone, use the recovery codes
  • Sign out of all sessions where the account offers it — this kicks out anyone already in
4Erase it — only when you are sureA wipe is permanent. It ends location reporting, so you give up any chance of getting the phone back. Do it when it is clearly stolen rather than mislaid.
  • Same screens as step 1: Erase This Device on iPhone, Erase Device on Android
  • On most phones the device stays locked to your account after a wipe, so it is still not usable by whoever has it
  • If it is a work phone, let IT make this call
📄
Write down what happened while it is fresh. When you last had it, where, what was signed in, and what you have done since. If this turns into an insurance claim or an incident report, that note is what it gets built from.
